# Harmaasalmi

## Core premise
Harmaasalmi is a small village beside a narrow inlet. The passenger ferry
Tavi makes the short crossing to the village landing; a longer road runs
around the inlet. A café, the local radio and a handful of regular people
make up most of an evening. The village carries on perfectly well without the
listener, who overhears it from one of two listening positions — the landing
or the café — and never affects it.

## Primary tensions
- Continuity against small change: the place has to be recognisable before a
  change in it can mean anything.

## Tone
Plain, cold, unhurried.

## Hard constraints
- World events precede their presentation. A fragment voices what its source
  knows or perceives at its observation time; it never creates events, alters
  decisions or grants a source knowledge.
- Generation may supply wording, conversational texture, delivery and
  harmless incidental detail. Nothing causal.
- Silence and absence are valid projections.
- A sound from the water is evidence: a boat, a horn or an engine out there
  appears only if the pack puts it there, however faint or ambiguous. Room
  texture (cups, chairs, a tap, voices at the tables) is free, and so is
  ordinary passing road traffic where the listening position can hear the road.
- Nobody is waiting for the listener and nothing is addressed to them.
- A speaker may be uncertain or mistaken within what they know; that never
  makes their statement true.
- No mysteries, hidden causes or cliffhangers: the place is ordinary.
- Timeline positions read B<sheet>.C<evening>.S<clock>: C1 is Thursday, C2
  Friday, C3 Saturday; S1912 is 19:12.
