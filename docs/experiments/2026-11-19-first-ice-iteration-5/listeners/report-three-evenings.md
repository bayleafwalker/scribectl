# Blind listener — three evenings, both positions

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-three-evenings.md`: fifteen
clock-stamped scripts from the final chain in order (Friday 20:00–20:10 is the
repaired draft-b), no cards, packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions as
iterations 2 to 4. Report as returned:

1. Rhythm: Evenings run in a loop. The café has cups and spoons, a coffee machine, the radio (accordion, then guitar, then fiddle, with dedications and notices), the kitchen door, a car on the road slowing for the bend, wind on the window, and customers paying and leaving ("Mind how you go"). Two men at the corner table bicker about a tractor/car, a roof, a tarp. The landing is wind, rope on the bollard, and water or slush on the pilings. Around 18:12 a horn sounds and the ferry docks. Staff and regulars use the same short phrases every night.

2. Change: The ferry stopped, and ice moved in.
   - Thursday 18:12-18:15: the ferry docks normally.
   - Thursday 18:37-18:44: slush on the pilings, the ramp lands on slush ("No."), and the crossing is abandoned. (High on this.)
   - Thursday 19:20: radio notice that the final crossing is cancelled.
   - Friday 18:27: radio says the ferry is "closed", with replacement transport because of ice at the ramp.
   - Friday 19:35: a bus drops passengers at the café.
   - Friday 20:05: a delivery driver does "the long way round."

   Confidence: high that the ferry stopped and a road bus replaced it. Medium that it's an ice story rather than a one-night problem; the Saturday landing is simply empty.

3. Saturday: Yes, clearly the same place. I recognised the two men at the corner table, the half-cup woman who isn't stopping, the radio dedications (Marjatta; Ossi again), "Watch the bend", the 19:35 bus with its brake hiss, and the landing's slush and rope. Without being told, I noticed the room was fuller, the landing had no ferry, and the men now talk about "the bus Monday". The Thursday ferry passengers' "Evening" is gone.

4. Saturday pulls: The corner men's "Started first time" / "Near enough" calls back to Thursday's "Second go" (the car/tractor). The "tarp" and roof talk calls back to Thursday and Friday. At 19:50 the older man says "it'll be the bus Monday", and I wondered about the morning crossing. The landing at 18:15 has one pair of footsteps and a paper on a post, and I wanted to hear the landing during the bus stop. The 20:12 "Ossi, for his daughter" recalls Ossi's Friday dedication to a neighbour. Saturday wasn't flat, but the pull is gentle.

5. Mostly overhearing. A few lines serve the listener:
   - "The ferry's closed. There's replacement transport in its place." / "Their reason: early ice at the landing has made the ramp approach unsafe." (the presenter spells out the cause, in the pattern of the Thursday notice)
   - "Twenty-five to, it should be. If you're waiting on it." / "Is the toilet through there?" (setup for the bus)
   - "A full room for a Saturday." (narrated, not heard)
   - "Dry and freezing. You don't go up a roof freezing."
   - "What's the sheet say for the morning one?" (plants the schedule)

   Overall: overhearing, with the radio doing some explaining.

6. Pacing: Within a fragment, time passes at its own speed, with sparse sounds and silence. The 10-minute blocks feel real, especially the Friday delivery and the Saturday closing (chairs up, mop, radio off), which runs unhurried and drags in a good way. The jumps between windows feel like selected highlights, though, because the heavy events (ferry, notice, bus) are all caught on the spot. Thursday 19:20 and Friday 18:25 are short and dense with news. The Saturday 19:50 corner-table talk feels over-busy with topic changes.

7. Inconsistencies:
   - Thursday 19:30 is a pulla count and "beans tomorrow", but Friday's 20:05 delivery doesn't mention beans.
   - Friday 19:32 "Twenty-five to" implies a bus at 19:35, yet Saturday's bus time was never established.
   - Saturday 19:50 has "a man's voice reading something" on the radio, but nothing was announced.
   - The older man's car "started first time" on Saturday, then "plays up in the cold" at 19:50, which are fine as banter.
   - Thursday 18:12 horn heard from the café, while the landing places it at 12:40.
