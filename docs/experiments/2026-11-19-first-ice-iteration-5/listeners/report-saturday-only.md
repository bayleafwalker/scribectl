# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
clock-stamped Saturday scripts from the final chain, told it had never tuned in
before. No cards, packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions as iterations 2
to 4. Report as returned:

1. It is a small, cold, quiet village. The ferry landing is wind across the inlet, slush against the ramp and pilings, a rope creaking on a bollard, and a bit of paper ticking on a post. The café is a Saturday evening room with a local radio programme of dedications and records, a coffee machine, kitchen clatter, and two men at a corner table bickering over a roof, a car and a shed. It closes up around 20:15 with chairs on tables and a mop.

2. Mostly ordinary, with possibly one small difference. The café starts "a full room for a Saturday". Then a bus-like vehicle arrives at 19:35 and several people with coats and "eight, nearer half past" come in. That looks like an arrival from the ferry or a bus, which seems to be a normal rhythm.

   The slush on the ramp, the frozen road, "dry and freezing" and the "morning one" sheet hint that something is up with the ferry. The landing's weather matters more than the people do. The older man's car "started first time. Near enough", and he insists on taking the bus Monday. The roof and shed keep getting put off. I can't say what has changed. My guess, low confidence, is that the cold or ice is disrupting the crossing or the road. Another guess, also low confidence, is that the older man is avoiding something. Evidence: the slush, "Watch the bend", the car slowing for the bend, and "Sunday's spoken for. Nothing. I said."

3. Mostly overheard. The talk is fragmentary and the two men repeat themselves in a believable way. A few lines felt aimed at me:
   - "Dry and freezing. You don't go up a roof freezing."
   - "That's what it said on it in the spring."
   - The presenter's "for the neighbours, for the loan of the shovel", though dedications do sound like that.

   The cue-sheet narration like "A full room for a Saturday" also explains a bit.

4. It felt like time passing in the café, especially 20:10 to 20:20, where the closing routine ran at its own pace and the mop and fridge hum carried on. The 19:30 to 19:40 stretch was busy and a bit crowded, with the vehicle arrival and many small events, so it felt like highlights. The landing was slow and repetitive in a pleasant way, though the same wind, slush and rope cues recycled with little to pin down. The gaps between fragments (18:20 to 19:30, 19:40 to 19:50, 19:55 to 20:10) were jumps, so I could not follow anyone through them.

5. Yes, I would tune in again. I would listen to the landing for ferry sounds. I never heard a ferry, a bell or an arrival, which is odd for a landing. I would also listen for the corner-table men, to learn whether the roof, the car and the "Sunday" get resolved. I'd also want to know who Marjatta, the neighbours and the shovel are, and whether the Monday bus or the "morning one" sheet matters.
