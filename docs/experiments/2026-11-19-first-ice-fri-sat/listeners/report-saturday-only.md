# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
Saturday scripts, told it had never tuned in before. No cards, packs, canon
or event sheet. Report as returned:

1. Place: a small lakeside or inlet village in winter, with a ferry landing and a café that doubles as the bus stop. The landing is cold and mostly empty: wind, slush grinding on the pilings, a stiff rope, one person hurrying past, one slow car. The café is warm and low-key: cups, spoons, a coffee machine, a radio with a presenter reading notices, small talk about errands. By 20:20 the staff close up, put the chairs on the tables and lock the door.

2. Something is different from usual. Medium-high confidence that the ferry is not running, probably for a few days, because of the ice. Evidence: the landing at 18:10 has slush and ice but no boat, no crowd and no engine, only one pair of footsteps; at 19:50 Customer 2 says "with the ferry off it's the long way round anyway", and people are planning bus trips for Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday; at 19:30 the bus arrives with several passengers ("Same as last night"), which suggests it carries people who would normally take the ferry, though I can't be sure; the café talks about the bus timetable and a return in the afternoon. Low confidence on how long it lasts. I can't tell whether the ferry stopped just now or some time ago.

3. It mostly felt like overhearing a place. The overlapping chatter, the half-heard radio and the closing routine felt natural. The one line that seemed to exist for me was "I was going to go Tuesday, but with the ferry off it's the long way round anyway, so —", which explains the situation more tidily than people usually talk. "Did you look at the timetable for coming back?" / "There's one in the afternoon. I wrote it down somewhere." is a mild case of the same thing. "He's got the other one till Tuesday" and "Same as last night" were fine, because they stay unexplained. The radio's "that one for Aino's lot ... the notices" is a nice hook I couldn't follow.

4. Yes, I would tune in again. I would listen at the landing for any sign of a boat (an engine, a horn, crew voices) and to the café for what the notices say, who Aino is, and who "he" is who has "the other one till Tuesday". I'd want to know whether the ferry comes back by Wednesday.
