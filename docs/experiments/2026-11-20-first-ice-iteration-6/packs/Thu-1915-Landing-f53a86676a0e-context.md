## Voice canon
**Preferred**
- A fragment is a real-time cue sheet over its whole observation window:
  what the ear receives at the listening position, cue by cue, stamped with
  clock time ([18:12:40]). Between cues the bed runs on unchanged. Cues are
  sparse (one every half minute or so is plenty) and a quiet minute stays
  quiet.
- Events sit at their recorded times: if the pack puts something at 18:12,
  the first sound of it comes at 18:12 here. Nothing of it is heard earlier,
  not even its approach. A time with seconds (S1812.40) is the second itself:
  the sound comes at [18:12:40], wherever it is heard.
- Sounds in square brackets; speech as short quoted lines tagged with the
  speaker's role.
- Ordinary first. The place sounds like itself before anything in it is
  noticed.
- Speech is overheard, not delivered: half-sentences, people talking to each
  other, not to the room.
- What everyone present already knows stays unsaid or half-said. It shows in
  what people do about it, never in a sentence that states it.
- When something does not happen, the script simply does not contain it.
- Distance stays distance: a sound from across the water is faint and partial.
- What is already heard here has been heard. People and routines recur in
  their own new words: the same habit, a different line on a different night.
  An earlier thread comes back only where it would come up anyway.
- Something that happens again on another evening (the same bus, the same
  closing-up) is heard afresh, not retraced. Its recorded time holds; what the
  ear catches around it, in what order and when, belongs to this evening. An
  earlier fragment is never a frame to re-dress.
- The bed recurs; its wording does not have to. Name each sound plainly, in
  this fragment's own words.

**Avoid**
- Exposition: a speaker telling someone what both already know, for the
  listener's benefit.
- Narration, scene-setting voice-over, or any line addressed to the listener.
- Dramatic sound design: thunder on cue, silences that "fall",
  music that swells.
- Foreshadowing and summing up.
- Naming moods or emotions; the ear cannot hear them.
- Replaying a line or a joke already heard here, or recapping it; the listener
  remembers. Rituals are the exception: greetings, sign-offs and the radio's
  set phrases recur the way places repeat them.
- Cue text that comments or interprets (who minds what, who reacts to what,
  what is usual or unusual for the place or the night): a cue is what the ear
  receives.
