# Blind listener — three evenings, both positions

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-three-evenings.md`: fifteen
clock-stamped scripts from the final chains in order (the landing from its
re-voiced chain), no cards, packs, canon or event sheet. The prompt is in
[prompts.md](prompts.md), unchanged since iteration 2. Report as returned:

1. Rhythm: a café evening runs on a loop. Cups and spoons, the coffee machine hissing, a car passing on the road every few minutes, the door letting in wind. The radio plays alternating accordion and guitar records. Every ten minutes or so the presenter reads a dedication (Sirkka, Raili, Ossi, Marjatta, Pentti). Two men at the corner table bicker about a roof, a ladder and a car. The kitchen clatters, the dishwasher hums, and someone says "Night, then". The landing is wind, a creaking rope and water under the planks, with a ferry arriving at about 18:13 on Thursday.

2. What changed: the ferry stopped, and the ice came in. I'm high confidence on that.
   - Thursday 18:10: ferry docks normally, with an engine, horn, ramp clang and Leena at the ramp.
   - Thursday 18:37-18:44: slush packs the pilings, and Leena can't seat the ramp ("No.").
   - Thursday 19:20: the radio announces tonight's final crossing is cancelled.
   - Thursday 19:31: the fridge has too little cream.
   - Friday 18:26: the radio says the ferry is closed because of ice, with a replacement bus.
   - Friday 19:35: the bus pulls up and fills the café.
   - Friday 20:05: the delivery van comes in by the back door.
   - Saturday: the landing is empty, and the café's bus crowd and the Monday-bus talk have become routine.

3. Saturday felt like the same place. I recognised the two men (roof, ladder, "brother's coming for the ladder"), the accordion and guitar records, the presenter and Pentti's dedications, the cars passing, the dishwasher hum, the kitchen and counter voices, and the 19:35 bus arrival. The things I noticed unprompted: the landing is silent, with no engine, no horn and nobody waiting. The bus is now a normal sound. The older man says "if it's the bus, it's the bus". The kitchen says "Milk's fine till Monday" and "Nothing left to keep" about the soup. The Saturday radio at 19:50 is only "a man talking, words lost".

4. Saturday did pull me in. The Friday bus arrival repeats at 19:35 almost beat for beat. That made me notice the café has absorbed the ferry's closure. I wanted to go back to the Thursday ice at the landing, and to hear whether the ferry returns. Saturday's landing fragment made me want to check whether anything is happening there at all. The roof banter ("You said this weekend, and then when it warms up") made me think of Thursday's "Saturday, then. The roof." There's also a hint that the older man's walk and car may be changing, since he's taking the bus and the car is "fine in July". The last fragment (closing up, bolt, radio cutting off mid-sentence) is quietly satisfying rather than flat.

5. Mostly overhearing. The radio notices are announcements, but they sound like real radio. Some lines seem to exist for the listener's benefit:
   - "Tonight's final crossing is cancelled."
   - "The ferry service is closed. There's early ice at the landing, and the operator says the ramp approach isn't safe."
   - "So that's from the operator. Back to the records."
   - "Full house."
   - "Dry and minus six. Nails won't take."
   - "Then no cream with the pie" (setting up the Friday delivery).
   - The presenter's "Only just come in" and "That's the notice".

6. Pacing: mostly real-time, in ten-minute windows with 20-40 second gaps between cues. The landing slush sequence on Thursday felt properly slow. The edges between fragments are a clear cut-together feel, since it jumps between evenings. The Friday 20:05 delivery drags slightly with its paperwork detail. The Saturday close-up is a bit tidy and complete, and reads like a conclusion.

7. Inconsistencies: small.
   - The "Sirkka dedication for her sister" appears on Thursday 18:08 as "with best wishes" and on Thursday 19:31 as "who's been poorly". It's plausibly a repeat, but it's the same dedication twice.
   - The café's radio at 19:50 is "a man talking", not the usual music.
   - Pentti's dedication for his wife is on Saturday 19:32 and for his brother at 20:13. Both are fine.
   - The Thursday 18:12 engine heard from the café is described as "low, uneven" and at the landing as "low, even note".
   - The older man says "Car started this morning. First go" on Thursday but is wary of the car by Saturday. That reads as development rather than a flaw.
