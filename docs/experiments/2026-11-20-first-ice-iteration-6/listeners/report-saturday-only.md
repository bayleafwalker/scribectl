# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
clock-stamped Saturday scripts from the final chains, told it had never tuned
in before. No cards, packs, canon or event sheet. The prompt is in
[prompts.md](prompts.md), unchanged since iteration 2. Report as returned:

1. A small waterside village in deep cold. The landing is wind, slush hissing along the pilings, a creaking mooring rope, paper notices flapping under their cover. The café is a warm, modest Saturday-evening place: coffee machine, crockery, radio (fiddle, accordion, dedications), soup, and two men at a corner table bickering mildly. It winds down and closes around 20:15.

2. Mostly ordinary, with a mild sense of something recent. Confidence: low.
   - Wind and minus six are a constant complaint, so it's a cold snap and the slush is new-ish.
   - The notices at the landing get one glance of attention, and the older man says "if it's the bus, it's the bus. I'm not fighting the car in this" and "What time's the first one, Monday? ... It's on the post." Together these suggest the ferry may not be running and a bus has replaced it. The bus arrives at 19:35 with a crowd of strangers, which fits that.
   - The woman who leaves as the bus arrives, "That's me, then", points the same way.

   I can't tell what the notices say or why. Nothing else feels disturbed.

3. It felt like overhearing a place. The talk is oblique, interrupted and about nothing in particular (ladder, roof, bag of fittings, gloves). The nearest to being for my benefit is "Dry and minus six. Nails won't take." Also the first dedication, "it's their anniversary on Sunday", and the matching second one, "for his brother, hoping the knee's mending." That second one is slightly neat, since it echoes the brother and the ladder. The soup lines ("Soup's ready", "Soup's done. Nothing left to keep.") are natural but they do bracket the evening a little tidily.

4. Real time within each window. The 19:30–19:40 stretch has a lot going on, including the bus arrival, but each beat has its own length and the room settles after. The 19:50 stretch is the best: slow, circling talk. Between windows there are big gaps (18:20 to 19:30, 19:55 to 20:10) with no sound. The landing is sparse and a bit samey: wind, slush, rope, repeated. It drags a little because nothing human happens except one pair of footsteps. The closing routine (chairs up, bolt, broom, till) is satisfying and unhurried. The bus arrival is the one moment that felt like a highlight.

5. Yes. I'd want to hear the landing at other times to learn whether the ferry runs, and to find out what the notices say. I'd listen for the bus again and for who gets off it. I'd follow the two men: the roof, the brother taking the ladder back on Sunday, Tuesday's van trip to the sister, and the bag of fittings by the door. I'd also listen to whether the café stays this quiet when the cold lifts.
