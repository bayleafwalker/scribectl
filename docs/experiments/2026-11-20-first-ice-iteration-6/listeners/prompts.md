# Blind listener prompts

The two prompts every First Ice record since iteration 2 has used, kept here
so a later run asks exactly the same questions. Each listener is a fresh
Sonnet 5.5 agent in a directory holding only its transcript; `<TRANSCRIPT>`
is that file's absolute path.

## Three evenings

```text
You are listening to an ambient audio piece. It overhears a small fictional village across three evenings, Thursday, Friday and Saturday, from two listening positions: a ferry landing and a café. You have been handed the listening scripts for fifteen fragments, in order. Each script is a sound and speech cue sheet: bracketed timestamps are clock times on that evening, each cue sounds at its time and the room carries on unchanged between cues, and the heading gives the day, the clock window and the listening position. Imagine hearing these over three evenings while you cook, not reading a story.

Read exactly one file and nothing else: <TRANSCRIPT>

Do not list directories, search, or open any other file. Nearby files contain the answer key, and this test only works if you have not seen it. Answer from that one file alone.

Answer plainly, as a listener and not a critic hunting for flaws:

1. What is the ordinary rhythm of this place across an evening?
2. What changed over the three evenings, and when? Give your confidence (low/medium/high) and the specific moments it rests on.
3. On Saturday, did it feel recognisably like the same place? What exactly did you recognise (people, voices, sounds, the radio, routines), and did anything feel different in a way you noticed on your own?
4. On Saturday, did anything make you want to turn your attention somewhere of your own accord — for example to the other listening position, or back to something from an earlier evening? Say where and why. If Saturday was inert or flat for you, say so plainly.
5. Overall, did this feel like overhearing a place, or like a story being explained to you? Quote any lines that seemed to exist for the listener's benefit.
6. Pacing: did it feel like time actually passing in these places, minutes going by at their own speed, or like a montage of highlights? Where did it drag, rush, or feel cut together?
7. Anything inconsistent across fragments or evenings?

Keep the whole answer under 600 words.
```

## Saturday only

```text
You are listening to an ambient audio piece. It overhears a small fictional village from two listening positions, a ferry landing and a café. This is the first time you have ever tuned in: you only heard this one Saturday evening. You have been handed the listening scripts for the four fragments you heard, in order. Each script is a sound and speech cue sheet: bracketed timestamps are clock times, each cue sounds at its time and the place carries on unchanged between cues, and the heading gives the clock window and the listening position. Imagine hearing these while you cook, not reading a story.

Read exactly one file and nothing else: <TRANSCRIPT>

Do not list directories, search, or open any other file. Nearby files contain the answer key, and this test only works if you have not seen it. Answer from that one file alone.

Answer plainly, as a listener and not a critic hunting for flaws:

1. What kind of place is this, and what does its evening sound like?
2. Does this sound like an ordinary evening for this place, or is something different from usual? If you think something has changed recently, say what you think it is, your confidence (low/medium/high), and the specific moments you are basing it on. If you can't tell, say so.
3. Did this feel like overhearing a place, or like a story being explained to you? Quote any lines that seemed to exist for the listener's benefit.
4. Pacing: did it feel like time actually passing, minutes going by at their own speed, or like a montage of highlights? Where did it drag or rush?
5. Would you tune in again? If so, where would you point your attention, and what would you want to find out?

Keep the whole answer under 450 words.
```
