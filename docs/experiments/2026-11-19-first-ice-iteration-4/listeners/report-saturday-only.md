# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
clock-stamped Saturday scripts from the final chain, told it had never tuned in
before. No cards, packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions as iterations 2
and 3. Report as returned:

1. This is a small, cold-weather village. The landing is wind, slush grinding on the pilings, and creaking ropes, with almost no people. The café is a warm, low-key local place. It has a radio with dedications and notices, a coffee machine, and a few regulars. A bus drops people off at 19:35. Then the room thins out and closes at 20:15.

2. It mostly sounds ordinary. Nobody reacts to anything unusual, and the closing routine is steady and practised. I can't clearly name a recent change, and my confidence in any change is low. Three threads might be hints of one:
   - The landing is nearly empty at 18:15 (two walkers), and the ferry never shows. The younger man says "Bus there and back, with the boat off" (19:54). That suggests the ferry isn't running, probably because of the slush or ice. It could be seasonal, or it could be new.
   - The older man keeps deflecting about his car ("It won't turn over till noon", "It's on the list"). That reads as a family habit, not a change.
   - The jar he says he'll bring "tomorrow" is oddly emphasised.

   I'd call the ferry being off the best candidate, at low to medium confidence.

3. It mostly felt like overhearing a place. The overlapping noises, half-heard words and the radio going under the machine all felt real. A few lines felt a little explanatory:
   - "Not tonight. We're eating at hers."
   - "Did you ring your brother back?" / "From the bus. He's not got the trailer till Tuesday."
   - "Bus there and back, with the boat off."
   - "You have to know what you're looking at before you touch anything."
   - "Soup's in the fridge… It'll do for lunch."

   The bus arrival and the walk to the car at 19:38 to 19:39 also felt a bit staged to show the road and the bus.

4. Time did pass at its own speed in the café windows. The closing sequence from 20:10 to 20:20 was the best part, with chairs, the till, the broom and the light switch. The landing windows were slow and a little samey, which fit the setting. The real rush is the gaps. Only about 25 minutes of a whole evening are shown, so it is a set of slices, not a continuous stream. The 19:30 to 19:40 slice is crowded with arrivals and felt a bit like a highlight reel. The 19:50 slice was quieter.

5. Yes, I'd tune in again. I'd listen at the landing to learn whether the ferry runs and whether the slush has anything to do with it. I'd listen at the café for the older man's car and the jar, and for what happens Sunday with the sister. I'd also like to hear the radio dedications again (Marjatta, Sirkka) and the place at a different hour, such as morning.
