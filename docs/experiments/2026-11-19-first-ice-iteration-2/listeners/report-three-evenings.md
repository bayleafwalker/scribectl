# Blind listener — three evenings, both positions

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-three-evenings.md`: fifteen
clock-stamped scripts in order (Saturday 19:50 is the repaired draft-b), no
cards, packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions as the Fri/Sat record plus
one on pacing. Report as returned:

1. Rhythm. The café has a steady loop: crockery, the coffee machine hiss, the radio (guitar, accordion and fiddle records, then a low dedication from the presenter), a car passing on the road, a kitchen tap and pan. A woman comes in for "just half" and leaves with "Mind how you go". Two men at the corner table bicker about a roof, a shed and a car that won't start in the cold. The landing has wind, water on the pilings, ropes creaking. In the early evening the ferry comes in, unloads, takes on passengers and leaves. Later it is quiet apart from wind and slush.

2. What changed. Confidence: high that the ferry stopped, medium on the timing.
   - Thursday 18:12 to 18:16: the last normal ferry docks and leaves.
   - Thursday 18:35 to 18:44: slush packs the landing and the ramp won't seat. Leena says "No." under her breath. This is where it starts.
   - Thursday 19:20: the radio announces tonight's last crossing is cancelled.
   - Thursday 19:30: the café kitchen says "I'll work round it" and the counter takes down the specials board, leaving the soup. Stock isn't arriving.
   - Friday: the landing is empty. The radio says the crossing is closed because of ice. At 19:35 a replacement bus brings passengers into the café.
   - Friday 20:05: a supplier delivers by the back door and says "Long, but all right."
   - Saturday: the bus has become routine and the supply route is settled.

3. Saturday. Yes, the same place. I recognised the roof and car banter, almost word for word from Thursday ("It's not weather for a roof", "It wasn't weather in August"). I also recognised the woman's "half", the accordion and guitar rotation, the "from all of us at home" dedication, and "Mind the road". What I noticed unprompted: the landing is deserted with paper ticking against a board, the bus arrives where the ferry used to, passengers say "Ring him from here" and "About an hour", someone says "Not with the ferry off, it'll be full of everybody", and the shed excuse has drifted from "August" to "September" to "after Christmas".

4. Saturday pull. Mildly yes. The lone quick footsteps at the landing (18:14) made me wonder where that person was going. In the café, the older man's "Take the bus... Go early" pointed back to the ferry loss. "Try her at ten tonight" on the car did too. The 20:15 closing routine, the radio clicking off and chairs going up, made me want to stay in the room. Nothing felt inert. It is quiet, but quiet in a lived-in way.

5. Overheard vs explained. Mostly overheard. The Friday radio notice reads as explanatory ("Their reason: early ice at the landing has made the ramp approach unsafe. That's what they've said."). The Thursday one is similar ("Tonight's final crossing is cancelled... That's as it came."). Both are plausible as radio, but they are plainly there to tell the listener. "Nothing yet." and "So do we —" worked better as overheard.

6. Pacing. Mostly real time. The ten-minute windows run at their own speed and the closing routine is nicely unhurried. The landing stretches (Thursday 19:05, Friday 18:10) are sparse and slightly drag, and they are all wind. Thursday 19:20 to 19:22 is a very short clip, so the radio notice feels cut in. The jumps between evenings are a montage of highlights, but they are acceptable.

7. Inconsistencies. Small ones:
   - The woman who leaves Thursday as "Long way round" has her car start on the second try, which is fine.
   - The curtains "back Saturday" never come up again on Saturday.
   - Thursday 18:12 puts the ferry's engine across the water, but the café horn is heard at 18:12:50, while the landing has the horn at 12:40. That is fine.
   - On Friday the woman's "car door, car starting" at 19:35 coincides with the bus arriving, which is clear.
   - Saturday 19:50 has the radio as "a man talking", which differs from the usual music.
   - The two men are labelled YOUNGER MAN/OLDER MAN on some evenings and FRIEND on others (Friday and Saturday 19:30). This is only cosmetic.
   - The Friday 19:30 "Tomorrow it'll be warmer" followed by Saturday's "warmer" talk is consistent.
