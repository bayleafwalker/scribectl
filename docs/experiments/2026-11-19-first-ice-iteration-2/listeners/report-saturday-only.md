# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
clock-stamped Saturday scripts (19:50 is the repaired draft-b), told it had
never tuned in before. No cards, packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions
as the Fri/Sat record plus one on pacing. Report as returned:

1. It sounds like a small, cold, wintry village with a ferry landing and a café on a road. The landing is wind, slush grinding on pilings, a creaking rope and paper ticking on a board. The café is cups, a coffee machine, a radio with records and dedications, and two men at the corner table bickering. By 20:15 it is closing: chairs go up, the broom comes out, the dishwasher starts.

2. Something is a bit different, with medium confidence. The ferry seems to be off. The landing is almost empty (one hurried walker at 18:14), and a man says "Not with the ferry off, it'll be full of everybody" (19:52:45). A bus has apparently replaced it. A bus pulls up at 19:34:45, and its passengers come in for coffee and a phone call ("About an hour, yes"). The "it's on the board, the times" line fits that too. I can't tell why the ferry is off. Slush and ice are the obvious guess, but that is only a guess. The roof and the car are a separate thread. They are just the older man's habit of putting things off: the roof is "still waiting", the car "won't go first thing", and he "said that in September" and "about the car". That sounds like ordinary life, not a change.

3. Mostly it felt like overhearing a place. The interrupted fragments, the radio dedication half lost under the cups and the "Half, was it?" exchange all felt real. A few lines felt slightly there for me:
   - "Not with the ferry off, it'll be full of everybody."
   - "It's on the board, the times."
   - "It wasn't weather in August either." and "You said that in September." These make the stalling joke obvious, and the same joke comes back three times.

   The Saturday-only framing also made the repetition of the roof argument feel a bit designed.

4. Mostly time passing. The landing's long gaps (nothing from 18:11:50 to 18:13:20, then just "Wind.") felt right. The closing-up at 20:15–20:20 was the best part, with its small routine sounds. The 19:30 fragment felt slightly busy. The bus arrival, the new passengers and the dedication all fall in about ten minutes, so it leans toward a highlight reel. The 19:50 fragment is slow and a little circular.

5. Yes, I'd tune in again. I'd listen to the landing for boats or people, and for whether the slush and wind ease. I'd also want to know why the ferry is off and when it comes back. At the café I'd check whether the roof or the car ever actually gets dealt with, and whether the buses keep bringing strangers in. I'd also listen for a weekday evening to see what the ordinary pattern is.
