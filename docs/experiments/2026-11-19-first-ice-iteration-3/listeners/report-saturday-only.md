# Blind listener — Saturday only

Fresh agent (Sonnet 5.5), given only `transcript-saturday-only.md`: the four
clock-stamped Saturday scripts, told it had never tuned in before. No cards,
packs, canon or event sheet. Same questions as iteration 2. Report as
returned:

1. A small, cold, wintry village. The landing is wind, slush shifting under the planks, a loose paper rattling in its frame and a strap knocking. The café is warm and low-key: an accordion and guitar radio show with dedications, coffee machine hiss, kitchen clatter, a few regulars murmuring. A bus stops outside and drops a few passengers. By 20:15 the café has closed up.

2. Mostly ordinary, but I think something small has changed. Confidence is medium that it is a transition for the two men and low-to-medium that it is anything bigger.
   - Younger man to older man: "Bed's made. Boxes are in the car." "Temporarily." This suggests the older man is moving in with the younger one, or is about to.
   - The Monday/Tuesday talk about the felt, the shed and the car starting in the cold suggests he is going back and forth between two places and not committing to a date. "We'll see about Tuesday."
   - "With the boat off it's the bus for everyone anyway." The ferry isn't running, which fits the slush and ice at the landing. That is the village-wide change. I think it is the cold, not a crisis.

   I can't tell whether the older man is leaving his own home or just staying over for a while.

3. Mostly overhearing. The overlapping fragments, the lost words ("something about ninety, the rest lost") and the cut-off lines ("Tuesday I'm — ") felt real. The weak spots are the lines that feel written to tell me things:
   - "With the boat off it's the bus for everyone anyway." That tells me the ferry is down more than anyone would bother to say.
   - "In the car." / "Temporarily." These are a bit pointed, though they work.
   - "Did you put the blanket over the engine?" / "It started." This repeats the setup of the later "She started Thursday." It is a little neat.

4. It felt like real time, with the fragments sitting at their own pace. The landing is slow, with long gaps of nothing but wind, which suits it. The café at 19:30 is busy and a little stuffed: the bus, the half-cup woman, the phone call and the passengers all arrive within ten minutes. That felt like a bundle of small events, though it was believable. The 19:50 fragment was the most natural. The closing-up at 20:10 was the most satisfying, with its practical details of chairs, key, bolt and broom. The jump between fragments is large, so I can't say anything about the gaps. The landing never has anyone speak, so it felt thin next to the café.

5. Yes. I'd listen to the café for the two men: does the older man move in, and does he take the bus on Tuesday? I'd also listen to the landing for whether the ferry runs again when the slush clears, and for the strap and the paper, which kept coming back. I'd like to know what the older man is doing on Tuesday and what's happening with his sister on Wednesday.
